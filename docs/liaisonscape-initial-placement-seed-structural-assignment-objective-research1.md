## 5. Hard-safety boundary

The exact floor remains:

- **GEO-0:** each accepted topology-derived motif is not exactly degenerate;
- **GEO-3:** distinct motif neighbors around a shared Node do not occupy the
  same positive directed ray.

These are constraints on a complete assignment, not preferences for a larger
altitude, wider angle, or shorter edge. No near-degeneracy threshold,
minimum-angle cutoff, epsilon repair, or scalar penalty is justified. If a
candidate family has no hard-valid mapping, that is a family-capacity result,
not a structural assignment failure.

## 11. Structural dominance and trade-off state

The following is a coherent research concept, not a schema or API:

```text
X ≽struct Y
```

`X` may structurally dominate `Y` only when:

1. both complete mappings satisfy the exact hard floor;
2. comparison uses the same motif/cluster identity and locality scope;
3. every descriptor admitted as a monotonic preference axis is no worse in X;
4. at least one admitted descriptor is strictly better in X; and
5. no non-monotonic descriptor exposes a contradictory structural state that
   makes the comparison unknown rather than better.

Condition 5 prevents a descriptive profile from being silently treated as a
score. When it cannot be discharged, the result is `UNKNOWN`, not dominance.
This makes dominance useful only as a conservative partial relation. It does
not yield a total rank and does not prescribe a search algorithm.

If neither mapping dominates, the result is `INCOMPARABLE / TRADE-OFF`. This
is an acceptable terminal state for hard-valid assignments.

## 12. Partial-preference model

A future conceptual model may use:

```text
X ≽struct Y
```

with these boundaries:

- **Hard prerequisite:** X and Y must be injective and satisfy GEO-0/GEO-3;
  family infeasibility is separate.
- **Eligible descriptors:** only descriptors explicitly accepted as
  monotonic, currently conditional motif chord relief and conditional local
  fan-out distribution.
- **Direction:** the direction must be declared for the exact local descriptor
  and comparison scope; no weight or threshold is allowed.
- **Identity/locality:** compare the same motif, shared Node, overlap
  cluster, or explicitly corresponding structural record.
- **Incomparability:** if one mapping improves one accepted record and the
  other improves another, return `INCOMPARABLE / TRADE-OFF`.
- **Topology-equivalent mappings:** preserve the orbit/class and do not give a
  named K3 Node a role merely to produce a literal representative.
- **Missing descriptors:** a missing or non-applicable descriptor is not zero;
  omit it from that scope and report the scope limitation.
- **Non-monotonic fields:** retain them as diagnostics and use them to mark a
  comparison `UNKNOWN` when they reveal a contradictory state.

This model is coherent as a partial research relation. It is not yet complete
enough for a bounded materializer experiment because the eligible descriptor
set, cross-motif handling, and unknown-state rules need a dedicated design.

## 13. Set-valued assignments and materialization

If several assignments remain structurally incomparable, a future semantic
description may retain a preferred/admissible assignment **set or class**.
This is a conceptual research direction only; no JSON, schema, API, or
persisted `PreferredAdmissibleAssignments` field is proposed here.

The objective and materializer remain separate:

```text
objective: determine hard-valid/admissible/preferred mapping relation
materializer: choose one literal representative after that relation exists
```

A materializer may use canonical order, lexical ID, or candidate index for
reproducibility only after semantic admissibility is established. Such a
tie-break is not a quality claim. K3 requires class/orbit handling before any
literal witness. A materializer is not allowed to repair an empty hard-valid
set with epsilon or settling.

The objective is also separate from a solver. This checkpoint designs no
backtracking optimizer, hill climbing, annealing, genetic algorithm, force
solver, ILP, SAT, CSP, or other search procedure. Finding a member is a later
operational concern.

The objective is separate from settling. A settling force may improve or
worsen the same descriptors, as the accepted Ashen and Lighthouse evidence
shows; that does not transfer settling ownership to assignment.

## 15. Crossings, edge length, compactness, and stability

### Crossings

Straight-line crossing count is a possible seed geometry diagnostic, but not a
current early objective. LiaisonScape routing can bend, avoid, or introduce
different visible crossings. Therefore straight-line crossings are a
**secondary proxy at most**, while routed crossing count is routing-owned. No
claim equates a straight-line count with final readability.

### Edge length

Total edge length is not primary. A local chord length may describe motif
scale, and an extreme edge may identify context, but shorter is not universally
better because compression can worsen chord, fan-out, node, or label pressure.
Edge length remains secondary/contextual.

### Compactness

Component/cluster extent and bbox are useful context, especially for viewport
use, but are trade-off-prone. Smaller extent can produce denser local sectors;
larger extent can create long edges and wasted space. Compactness is therefore
profile/context evidence, not an early assignment axis.

### Stability

Assignment stability and structural quality are separate. Topology-aware
policies improved rename and input-order stability in the preceding run, but
that does not prove their profiles are good. Incremental stability after a
graph edit is a later placement/settling concern and is **SEPARATE / LATER**,
not required for this initial-placement objective contract.

## 16. Candidate-family responsibility

The FP1 family remains an open capacity/geometry limit, not an automatic
failure. Its one-sided parabola has non-equivalent candidate roles and can
produce concentrated angular sectors and different profile classes under
correspondence reversal. That makes the assignment objective more complex,
but the evidence does not show that no meaningful partial relation can exist.

The correct distinction is:

- **Assignment-objective failure:** no non-arbitrary partial relation can
  compare any eligible local descriptors without hidden choices; versus
- **Candidate-family failure:** the fixed family makes structurally useful
  assignments impossible or creates unavoidable contradictory bottlenecks.

The current result is neither failure. Partial-preference semantics must be
designed first. If the next bounded objective experiment shows that every
topology-aware correspondence remains contradictory because FP1 cannot supply
enough compatible roles, candidate-family research may resume. No F2 design or
implementation is authorized here.

High-R-9 is **OPTIONAL** for a later objective experiment: its capacity-versus-
distribution contrast is useful, but Ashen already provides the required
overlap trade-off evidence. It was not rerun here.
