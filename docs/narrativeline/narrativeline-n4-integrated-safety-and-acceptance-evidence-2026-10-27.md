## Same-tab reload and Dataset protection

The [Dataset Replacement Safety design](../cross-app/dataset-replacement-safety-design.md) distinguishes `datasetModified`, `pendingUserWork`, and recoverable session state. An in-app Candidate replacement uses a guarded decision when modified or pending; an actual page exit, including reload, is protected by browser-native conditional `beforeunload`, not the application's replacement dialog. The [Cross-Context Workspace Safety design](../cross-app/cross-context-workspace-safety-design-2026-10-10.md) requires a recipient-owned Handoff and refusal of writes that could lose unresolved peer work.

The prior isolated Browser Use run saw Current A with `data-dataset-modified="true"`, then changed the **same tab's** fragment to a Cedar JA Handoff URL and called browser reload. It observed a Handoff-loading Home followed by Cedar JA as Current with 12 Events and its two correct warnings, without observing the in-app replacement dialog. It did not capture whether a browser-native `beforeunload` prompt appeared, was accepted, or was handled by Browser Use. It also did not capture a post-replacement copy of A. Therefore **data loss is plausible but unconfirmed**, and the browser-native exit protection is **INCONCLUSIVE**, not PASS or FIXED. This is not a normal Hub new-tab journey.

Source at that earlier checkpoint explains a material part of the transition: NarrativeLine saves changed in-memory Dataset content to `narrativeline.lastDataset` when safe to write. On startup it imports that stored Dataset and initializes `acceptedDatasetBaseline` from the same stored content. It then fetched startup `datasetUrl`; if `datasetModified` and `pendingUserWork` were both false, `handleOpenDataset` immediately called the guarded acceptance path. Thus a saved-but-not-exported title edit could appear clean after reload and be replaced by the Handoff without an additional in-app decision. The previous browser observation is consistent with that former source path. It is **not** proof that the browser skipped its exit prompt; `beforeunload` was registered when in-memory loss risk was true, and its existing unit test confirmed registration. Browser-native exit permission, local persistence, and explicit Dataset acceptance are separate safety boundaries. The prior A was disposable and its recovery from an Export or other copy was not established.

At this investigation checkpoint, Human still needed to decide whether a restored Dataset required an in-app Handoff decision. Human subsequently adopted that safety direction; the bounded correction and later Human browser results are in the [startup Handoff protection record](narrativeline-startup-handoff-persisted-dataset-protection-2026-10-27.md). The earlier observations and uncertainties above remain historical evidence, not the current Runtime status. Browser-native leave-prompt behavior and byte-identical retention were not established by the later review.
