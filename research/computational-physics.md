# Computational Physics

This document explores whether large collections of E2R datasets exhibit properties analogous to physical systems.

Unlike the Core Specification, this document is exploratory and non-normative.

Nothing in this document is required by E2R.

---

# Motivation

As information systems grow, they begin to exhibit large-scale behavior that is difficult to predict from individual objects alone.

This document explores whether concepts from physics provide useful metaphors—or even practical models—for understanding such systems.

---

# Computation as Dynamics

An E2R dataset is static while stored.

Its dynamics emerge through computation.

Applications, users, and automated processes continuously create, modify, interpret, and exchange datasets.

From this perspective, computation becomes the mechanism that produces observable change.

---

# Time

The Core does not define a universal flow of time.

Instead, time exists only where represented by Extensions or application behavior.

Different applications may therefore observe different notions of temporal progression.

---

# State Transitions

A dataset may be viewed as a sequence of states.

Each modification transforms one valid state into another.

The collection of possible transitions forms a state space.

Understanding this space may help explain long-term evolution.

---

# Locality

Most modifications affect only a small portion of a dataset.

This locality enables scalable editing, synchronization, and collaboration.

Large structural changes are often composed of many localized operations.

---

# Conservation

Certain properties may remain invariant despite structural changes.

Examples include:

- object identity
- historical meaning
- logical consistency
- referential integrity

Determining which properties should be preserved is an important research topic.

---

# Emergence

Large collections of simple objects may exhibit complex behavior.

Patterns such as hierarchies, clusters, conventions, and standards may emerge without centralized planning.

Emergent structure is expected to become increasingly important as interoperable datasets grow.

---

# Scale

Behavior observed in a small dataset may differ significantly from behavior observed across millions of datasets.

Research should therefore consider multiple scales, including:

- individual objects
- single datasets
- collections of datasets
- ecosystem-wide behavior

---

# Limits

Every computational system has practical limits.

Examples include:

- storage capacity
- processing time
- network latency
- synchronization cost
- human comprehension

These constraints influence the evolution of information systems in much the same way that physical laws constrain natural systems.

---

# Research Questions

- Can information systems exhibit emergent physical properties?
- Which invariants remain stable during long-term evolution?
- How does locality influence scalability?
- Can computational limits explain structural evolution?
- Are there universal principles governing large collections of interoperable datasets?

These questions remain open for future research.
