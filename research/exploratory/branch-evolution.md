# Branch Evolution

This document explores how E2R datasets may evolve through branching, merging, and distributed collaboration.

Unlike the Core Specification, this document is exploratory and non-normative.

Nothing in this document is required by E2R.

---

# Motivation

Most version control systems treat branching as a software engineering technique.

This document instead considers branching as a fundamental mechanism for knowledge evolution.

An E2R dataset may continue to exist in multiple valid forms simultaneously.

Rather than identifying a single authoritative version, branching allows different interpretations, perspectives, and research directions to coexist.

---

# Divergence

A dataset may branch for many reasons.

Examples include:

- alternative historical interpretations
- competing taxonomies
- different editorial policies
- independent research
- localized adaptations
- experimental Extensions

None of these branches are necessarily incorrect.

Each represents a different evolutionary path.

---

# Merge

Independent branches may later converge.

Merge does not necessarily produce a single result.

Different merge strategies may generate different valid datasets.

Conflicts should not always be interpreted as errors.

Sometimes conflicting knowledge reflects genuine differences in perspective.

---

# Lineage

Every dataset has a history.

Rather than storing only the current state, future systems may preserve the lineage of a dataset.

Possible lineage information includes:

- parent datasets
- merge history
- branching points
- structural transformations

The Core does not define such mechanisms.

---

# Distributed Evolution

Branching becomes particularly interesting in decentralized environments.

Independent participants may evolve datasets without requiring a central authority.

Synchronization becomes optional rather than mandatory.

Different communities may therefore develop different knowledge structures while remaining partially interoperable.

---

# Federation

Collections of related datasets may form federations.

Within a federation:

- datasets remain independent
- interoperability is preserved through the Core
- Extensions may differ
- synchronization policies may vary

Federation does not imply uniformity.

---

# Peer-to-Peer Knowledge

Future E2R ecosystems may operate without centralized repositories.

Datasets could be exchanged directly between peers.

Possible characteristics include:

- decentralized discovery
- distributed synchronization
- local ownership
- independent evolution
- selective replication

In such systems, knowledge spreads through networks rather than centralized services.

---

# Ecosystem

A mature E2R ecosystem may consist of countless interconnected datasets.

Some datasets merge.

Some permanently diverge.

Some disappear.

Others become influential and inspire entirely new branches.

Evolution therefore occurs at the ecosystem level rather than within a single dataset.

---

# Research Questions

- How should dataset lineage be represented?
- Can multiple branches remain equally valid?
- What kinds of merge strategies preserve meaning?
- How can decentralized datasets discover each other?
- Can interoperability emerge without central coordination?
- What defines the identity of a dataset after many generations of branching?

These questions remain open for future research.
