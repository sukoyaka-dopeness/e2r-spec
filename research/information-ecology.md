# Information Ecology

This document explores the possibility that collections of E2R datasets form an information ecosystem.

Unlike the Core Specification, this document is exploratory and non-normative.

Nothing in this document is required by E2R.

---

# Motivation

Most information systems assume isolated datasets.

E2R instead encourages interoperability through a shared Core and independent Extensions.

As more datasets become compatible, they may begin to interact as members of a larger ecosystem.

This document explores that possibility.

---

# Ecosystem

An information ecosystem consists of multiple independent datasets.

Each dataset has its own purpose, history, and maintainers.

Datasets are not required to share identical structures.

Instead, they communicate through compatible Core objects and mutually understood Extensions.

---

# Diversity

Diversity is a desirable property.

Different datasets may represent the same domain in different ways.

Different applications may also introduce different Extensions.

Rather than enforcing complete uniformity, E2R encourages coexistence.

A diverse ecosystem is often more resilient than a homogeneous one.

---

# Cooperation

Datasets may cooperate in many ways.

Examples include:

- importing compatible data
- sharing Extensions
- referencing external datasets
- exchanging schemas
- synchronizing selected objects

Cooperation does not imply centralization.

Each dataset remains independently managed.

---

# Competition

Different representations may compete.

Some Extensions may become widely adopted.

Others may disappear through lack of use.

Competition is expected to emerge naturally without requiring centralized control.

---

# Adaptation

Datasets may gradually adapt to their environment.

For example:

- adopting common Extensions
- simplifying incompatible structures
- improving interoperability
- reorganizing internal representations

Adaptation may occur through human decisions, software tools, or future automated systems.

---

# Resilience

A healthy ecosystem should tolerate diversity and failure.

The disappearance of one dataset should not invalidate the remaining ecosystem.

Likewise, incompatible Extensions should not prevent Core interoperability.

This resilience is one of the motivations behind the minimal Core philosophy.

---

# Long-Term Evolution

As the ecosystem grows, new conventions may emerge.

Some Extensions may evolve into de facto standards.

Others may eventually become official specifications.

This process should occur through widespread adoption rather than centralized planning.

---

# Research Questions

- What characteristics define a healthy information ecosystem?
- How can interoperability emerge without central control?
- Which kinds of Extensions naturally become common?
- How should incompatible evolutionary paths coexist?
- Can datasets form stable ecological relationships?

These questions remain open for future research.
